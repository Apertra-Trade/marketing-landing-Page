The market: five regions, one mesh

The mesh changes the ceiling again, because it changes what one customer is worth. Canada alone topped out around $100M ARR; a five-region hub-and-spoke model pointed to $305.8M in Year 5. As a full mesh, one account covers up to four destination markets instead of one, so average trade volume per customer rises sharply: $377.6M ARR in Year 5 and $874.4M in the Year 7 ceiling case, on the same customer counts and the same 1.25% blended take-rate.

Five-region sizing, using the checked figures: Canada to US surpassed $1 trillion CAD in 2024 (US takes 75.9% of Canada's exports), Canada to EU reached $134B in 2025, up 77% since CETA (EU is 7.9% of Canada's trade), plus US to EU, the largest corridor in the world. US small firms did $648.5B in exports (35.7% of identified exports); EU SMEs are 39.4% of intra-EU and 28.6% of extra-EU export value. That gave about $950B of corridor SAM. AU/NZ add about $50B: Australia's $652B total goods trade in 2024 is dominated by mining, so the AU figure here uses the non-resource SME lens (food, wine, beauty, agtech), not a share of bulk commodities. NZ SMEs are 97% of enterprises. Corridor SAM therefore rises to about $1.0T. Pitch the corridor number, it is defensible. If you count SMEs based in the five regions trading with the rest of the world too, home-market SAM rises to about $4.8T+. The mesh does not change SAM; it changes capture per customer, because the same business can now export and import in several directions on one account.

Why the model compounds

1
Retention above 130% is plausible, and the mesh is whyOne account covers up to four destination markets, plus importing. Revenue grows when a customer adds a direction, a buyer, or a second country, without a new sale. That is the wallet-share lift behind the higher average GTV in the mesh model.
2
Fast payback per shipmentOne $50,000 shipment at 1.25% returns $625 in trade fees, credited against that month's membership, covering acquisition in 3 to 6 months.
3
Buyers bring buyers, across regionsEach exporter invites 3 to 10 foreign buyers onto the rails to track, clear and settle, giving warm leads in the US, EU, AU and NZ with near-zero marketing spend. Revenue also grows automatically when a customer adds a second corridor.

Revised mesh model, same method throughout, on the hybrid pricing: exporter membership net of the monthly fee credit (buyers and importers are free members and are not counted in the membership line; blended about $600/month retained per paying exporter account for US/CA/EU, AU/NZ blended slightly lower on entry) plus a blended 1.25% take-rate on every trade (0.75% FX and escrow combined + about 0.50% freight). Because membership is credited against trade fees, the two lines are the retained total per account, not a double charge. Why the GTV per customer jumps versus the hub-and-spoke version: one account now covers up to four destination markets instead of one, so wallet share per customer rises. Year 2 stays at $9.6M ARR: the mesh builds as regions launch in sequence, but the product is mesh from the architecture, so no rebuild is needed when a new region opens. Year 5: $20.25B GTV is 15,000 × $1.25M in US/CA/EU ($18.75B) plus 2,500 × $600K in AU/NZ ($1.5B); take at 1.25% is $253.1M, plus membership of $124.5M net of credits, equals $377.6M ARR. Year 7: $49.5B GTV is 30,000 × $1.5M ($45B) plus 6,000 × $750K ($4.5B); take $618.8M plus membership $255.6M net of credits equals $874.4M ARR. Treat as a scenario to test in customer interviews, not a forecast.
